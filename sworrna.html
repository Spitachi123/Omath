Meet the mountain

A granite giant at the mouth of Guanabara Bay

Sugarloaf Mountain is a striking natural landmark overlooking Rio de Janeiro, at the entrance to Guanabara Bay in southeastern Brazil. In Portuguese it is called Pão de Açúcar, named for its shape, which resembles the traditional loaf of refined sugar used in the 19th century.

This tall granite cone with a circular top is a spectacular part of Rio.

Fact A · Rock climbing

A Climber’s Playground

Sugarloaf is one of the largest and most popular urban rock-climbing destinations in the world. With 270 different routes, you can climb high above the Atlantic Ocean and the sprawling city of Rio de Janeiro, or simply watch other adventurers conquer the iconic mountain.

Fact B · Neighbours

Neighbours Worth Visiting

It is not the only unique landmark in the area. Its neighbours add to the tranquil beauty of the whole peninsula. Morro de Babilonia (Babylon Mountain) and Morro de Urca (Urca’s mountain) are accessible to the public and open to rock climbers. Morro de Urca even has a play area for children and a restaurant.

Fact D · The bay

The Bosom of the Sea

Historic Guanabara Bay stretches out in front of Sugarloaf Mountain. Its name translates as “the bosom of the sea”. In the 16th century it was home to several fortifications built by the colonial government. Today the bay is made up of more than 130 islands and is surrounded by beautiful scenery that you can see from high above as you tour the mountain.

Fact E · Cable cars

Ride the Glass Cable Cars

Two cable cars take visitors to the summit, where you can enjoy stunning views of Rio de Janeiro from a height of 1,296 feet. The cars themselves are enclosed by glass panels. As you begin to ascend to the very top, breathtaking views unfold below you.
