Section 6.3

Conservation of Endangered & Rare Plants

Species such as Jatamasi, Champ, Sarpagandha, Panchaule and Lauth Salla are declining rapidly from unsustainable harvesting, habitat degradation and over-exploitation — Nepal answers with law, protected land, and careful science.

Control of International Trade of Endangered Wild Fauna and Flora Act, 2073 (2017)

Nepal's national law enforcing plant and wildlife trade protection, aligned with CITES — the Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species.

Five species under pressure

Nepal's most threatened flora

These plants share the same story: high demand for traditional medicine, slow growth, and habitats that are shrinking fast.

जटामसी

Jatamasi

Nardostachys jatamansi

An alpine herb harvested for its aromatic rhizome, used as incense and sedative — over-collection above 3,000m has made it critically endangered.

सर्पगन्धा

Sarpagandha

Rauvolfia serpentina

Its root yields reserpine, used to treat high blood pressure — a medicinal value that has driven it to the edge of extinction in the wild.

पञ्च औंले

Panchaule

Dactylorhiza hatagirea

A high-altitude orchid whose palm-shaped tuber is prized as a tonic — illegal collection has pushed it onto CITES Appendix II.

Champ Michelia champaca — fragrant timber tree, felled faster than it regrows
Lauth Salla Taxus wallichiana — Himalayan yew, source of a cancer-drug compound

Protecting plants where they grow

In-Situ Conservation

Keeping species inside their natural habitat, so seed dispersal and ecological interactions continue undisturbed.

Protected areas

National parks, wildlife reserves and conservation areas protect plant species directly inside their original ecosystems.

Controlling fire & overgrazing

Preventing wildfire outbreaks and managing cattle grazing preserves undergrowth, letting saplings regenerate naturally.

Community forestry

Local communities manage and protect forests directly — one of Nepal's most successful decentralised models, restoring millions of hectares.

Backed by science and the law

Ex-Situ Conservation & Legal Protection

Conservation outside the natural habitat — cultivated, studied, and protected by enforcement and public awareness.

Botanical gardens

Seeds and saplings of rare species are collected and cultivated in controlled sites like the Central Botanical Garden, Godavari.

Tissue culture

Micropropagation mass-produces thousands of disease-free saplings rapidly from a single parent plant.

Strict laws

Enforced anti-poaching and anti-smuggling laws deter illegal collection, transport, and international trade of protected flora.

Public awareness

Educating communities on the ecological and economic value of sustainable harvesting reduces destructive exploitation.

Section 6.4

Conservation Measures for Wildlife

The Bengal Tiger, One-horned Rhinoceros and Red Panda face severe survival threats from poaching, human-wildlife conflict, and fragmented habitats — here's how Nepal is fighting back.

Three flagship species

Icons of the Himalayan lowlands and hills

Bengal Tiger

Panthera tigris tigris

Nepal's apex predator of the Terai grasslands — numbers are recovering in Chitwan and Bardia thanks to strict anti-poaching enforcement.

One-Horned Rhino

Rhinoceros unicornis

Once down to a few hundred, Chitwan's rhino population has rebounded through zero-poaching years and habitat management.

Red Panda

Ailurus fulgens

A shy bamboo-forest dweller of the eastern hills, threatened by logging and fragmentation of its cool, mossy habitat.

Protecting animals in the wild

In-Situ Conservation

Protected areas

Chitwan National Park — Nepal's first — and Bardia National Park give rhinos, tigers and wild elephants room to roam.

Anti-poaching patrols

Nepal Army personnel and forest guards use camera traps, acoustic monitoring, and real-time SMART patrolling.

Habitat management

Controlled grass burning, artificial waterholes, and ecological corridors keep migration routes open and safe.

Safeguarded outside the wild

Ex-Situ Conservation

Zoological parks

The Central Zoo in Jawalakhel raises and cares for species in a protected environment, doubling as public education.

Breeding centers

Dedicated centers for gharials in Chitwan, and Vulture Safe Zones, raise critically endangered species for release into the wild.

The human side of conservation

Community Involvement & Research

Study & research

Wildlife surveys, radio-collaring and population censuses track health, reproduction, movement, and threat levels.

Awareness & ecotourism

Community programs encourage sustainable ecotourism — home-stays and guided safaris — returning revenue to local households.

Human-wildlife conflict

Predator-proof corrals, solar-powered fencing, and compensation programs reduce crop damage and livestock loss.

Medicinal Plants of Nepal: Nature's Gift for Health

From tropical Terai plains to alpine Himalayan peaks, Nepal's altitudinal range creates habitats for a vast repository of medicinal plants — Karnali province alone shows exceptional diversity in wild medicinal flora.

0

flowering plant species in Nepal have recorded medicinal use — one of the richest pharmacopoeias of any country its size.

Four everyday healers

Plants you'll find in almost any Nepali household

यार्सागुम्बा

Yarsagumba

Ophiocordyceps sinensis

A parasitic fungus-caterpillar complex from alpine grasslands (3,000–5,000m) — among Nepal's most valuable, and strictly regulated, forest products.

  • Energy booster — eases fatigue, boosts stamina
  • Traditionally used to support immunity
  • High market value makes strict regulation essential

तितेपाती

Mugwort

Artemisia vulgaris

A common perennial herb across mid-hill pastures, roadsides and wasteland throughout Nepal — abundant well beyond its role as a wayside weed.

  • Natural insect repellent and pest deterrent
  • Extracts used in soaps and essential oils
  • Dried foliage used as bedding and compost

घिउकुमारी

Aloe Vera

Aloe barbadensis

A succulent with thick, fleshy, serrated leaves filled with clear gel — one of the most versatile plants grown at home.

  • Soothes sunburn, acne and speeds wound healing
  • Strengthens hair roots, reduces dandruff
  • Rich in Vitamins A, C and E

तुलसी

Tulsi

Ocimum sanctum

A sacred aromatic plant grown across homes and temples throughout Nepal — revered in Hindu tradition and prized for everyday medicine.

  • Eases coughs, colds, asthma and sore throat
  • Adaptogenic — lowers stress and fever
  • Potent antibacterial and antiviral properties
