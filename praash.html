Reading I — picture analysis

Before the words — what does the scene tell us?

❓
What are the people doing?
The adult villagers are shouting, arguing, and pushing each other in the street over a minor conflict between two little girls. Meanwhile, the girls have already reconciled and are playing together peacefully.
⚖
Why is it good or bad?
It is bad — adults are letting pride and anger turn a harmless childhood incident into a foolish street fight. It highlights adult foolishness compared to childhood innocence and forgiveness.
Part 1 of 5

Meeting at the Puddle

It was early Easter in the village; melting snow formed dirty water streams running down the street.

Two little girls, Akoulya (elder) and Malasha (younger), met near a large puddle between two farmyards.

Both girls were dressed in new festive frocks and red head-scarves after coming from church, and began showing off their fine clothes to each other.

Part 2 of 5

The Accidental Splash

Malasha wanted to wade into the puddle. Akoulya warned her to take off her shoes and stockings first to avoid a scolding.

As they stepped into the deep water, Akoulya cautioned Malasha to walk carefully without splashing.

However, Malasha accidentally plumped her foot down hard, splashing dirty mud directly onto Akoulya's new dress and face.

Enraged by her ruined dress, Akoulya chased a frightened Malasha to strike her.

Part 3 of 5

Conflict Escalates Among Adults

Akoulya's mother saw the stained dress, scolded her daughter, and struck Malasha on the neck when she blamed her.

Malasha cried loudly, bringing her mother out to angrily challenge Akoulya's mother.

The argument between the two women escalated into a loud, angry neighborhood quarrel.

Men joined in, shouting and shoving without listening, bringing the minor incident near physical blows.

Part 4 of 5

Peacemaking & Reconciliation

Akoulya's grandmother stepped into the crowd, pleading for peace on a joyful Easter day, but the angry crowd ignored her.

While the adults continued shouting, Akoulya wiped the mud off her dress and began digging a channel with a stone to drain the puddle.

Malasha joined her with a wooden chip to help dig the channel.

Forgetting their conflict completely, both girls joyfully watched the water stream down the street together.

Part 5 of 5

Adult Shame & Resolution

The water stream carried the wooden chip right toward the group of fighting men.

The two little girls ran alongside the stream, laughing happily and chasing their toy together.

Grandmother pointed out to the men that the children had already forgiven each other while the adults were still fighting.

Ashamed of their foolishness, the men laughed at themselves and peacefully returned to their homes.

The moral of the story
“

Except you turn, and become as little children, you shall in no wise enter into the kingdom of heaven.

— Closing reflection by Leo Tolstoy

1. Quick Forgiveness
Children do not hold grudges. Forgiving minor mistakes quickly prevents unnecessary conflict and restores peace.
2. Child Wisdom vs. Adult Ego
Adults often let pride and ego ruin relationships, whereas children prioritize joy, play, and togetherness.
3. Purity of Heart
True spiritual purity and peace come from letting go of anger and living with innocent, loving hearts.
