Section One

What Is an E-mail?

An e-mail, or electronic text message, or e-text, or electronic mail, is a system which enables us to convey our information, message, matters, ideas, or experiences with others in digital form. It is one of the fastest ways of communication.

Section Two

Types of Emails

Type 1

Informal Emails

These are usually written to friends, people you know, or family members. They are very much like informal letters. In these emails, perfect grammar and correct spelling are not as important.

Type 2

Formal Emails

These are written to bosses, companies, teachers, professors, or official groups. They use serious language without short forms like "don't" or "can't." They are like official letters, and correct grammar is very important.

Section Three

Tone

Email tone reflects your attitude toward the recipient.

Formal Recipients

Use a respectful, serious, and direct tone for formal recipients — teachers, bosses, organizations.

Informal Recipients

Use a warm, friendly, and casual tone for informal recipients — friends, family.

Section Four

Formality & Politeness

Every email must be polite.

Formal Emails Require

  • 1Standard greetings (Dear Sir/Madam)
  • 2Respectful closing phrases (Sincerely)
  • 3Full words without contractions (cannot, will not)
  • 4No slang or emojis

Informal Emails Allow

  • 1Short forms (can't, I'm)
  • 2Casual endings (Best, Take care)

Section Five

Key Rules for Email Writing

  • aCheck the Layout: Always include a greeting, proper layout, good language, and a polite ending.
  • bAvoid Short Forms: Do not use contracted words like don't, can't, or I'm in formal emails — write do not, cannot, or I am instead.
  • cWrite Clear Subject Lines: Use short and informative subject lines instead of single vague words like Urgent or Enquiry.
  • dNo Slang or Shortcuts: Do not use informal words or text shortcuts like gonna, U, or 2.
  • eDo Not Use ALL CAPS: Avoid typing entire sentences in CAPITAL LETTERS because it looks like shouting.

How to Write an Email — Part 1

The Header Section

1 To

Enter the primary recipient's exact email address. Ensure there are no spelling errors in the address.

2 Cc / Bcc

Use Cc (Carbon Copy) to keep people informed who do not need to reply. Use Bcc (Blind Carbon Copy) to keep recipients' email addresses private from one another.

3 Subject Line

Write a short, clear, and specific topic summary.

ExampleAbsence Application for Grade 10 – Raman Ghimire

How to Write an Email — Part 2

The Body Section: Opening

1 Salutation / Greeting

Address the reader respectfully based on your relationship.

FormalDear Sir/Madam, Dear Mr. Sharma, Dear Principal
InformalHi Ramesh, Hello Everyone

2 Opening Sentence

State the main reason for writing immediately.

FormalI am writing to inform you that… / I am writing to request…
InformalHope you are doing well! / I wanted to check in about…

How to Write an Email — Part 3

The Body Section: Core Message

3 Main Text / Context

Keep sentences focused on the main topic. Avoid short forms (don't, can't), slang, emojis, or writing in ALL CAPS for formal emails.

4 Call to Action / Closing Request

Mention what step or response you expect next.

ExamplePlease let me know if you need any further details. / I look forward to hearing from you.

How to Write an Email — Part 4

The Body Section: Closing

5 Sign-off / Ending Phrase

End with a polite closing statement on a new line.

FormalSincerely, Best regards, Yours faithfully
InformalWarm wishes, Take care, Best

6 Name

Provide your full name, designation, or class/roll number at the very bottom.

In Summary

Write With Clarity, Write With Courtesy

A well-written email is polite, clear, and correctly structured, from subject line to sign-off.

Tone

Match your language to your recipient — respectful and direct, or warm and casual.

Structure

Header, salutation, opening, body, call to action, sign-off, and name.

Etiquette

Avoid short forms, slang, and ALL CAPS in formal correspondence.
