What is ICT?

Information and Communication Technology (ICT) refers to the tools, systems and technologies used to store, process, share and communicate information. It includes computers, mobile phones, the internet, software applications, networks and other digital devices.

SIGNALS

Analogue vs Digital signal

Analogue signal

A signal whose value changes continuously over time. It can take any value within a given range, like a smooth sine wave. Example: sound from a microphone.

Digital signal

Information in binary numbers: 0 and 1 (a bit). Changes in distinct steps or pulses: 1 = ON, 0 = OFF. Easy for electronic devices to read, process and transmit.
ADC (Analogue-to-Digital Converter): converts analogue → digital. Microphone → ADC → digital system.
DAC (Digital-to-Analogue Converter): converts digital → analogue. Digital system → DAC → speaker.
🎤 Analogue input➜ADC➜Digital signal processor➜DAC➜🔊 Analogue output

TRANSMISSION

Transmission of a digital signal

Source➜Coder➜Modulator➜Channel ⚡noise➜Demodulator➜Decoder➜Destination
Source: generally sources of signals are sound waves, analogue signals etc. for digital signals.
Encoder (Coder): minimizes the number of bits, making data compatible with bandwidth for transmission.
Modulator: modulates data as required for the channel; the digital signal becomes an analogue signal.
Channel: the medium or way (wire, air, fibre) carrying the signal to the receiver.
Demodulator: first step on the receiver side; converts the signal back to a form the decoder can read.
Decoder: digitizes the signal again, removes noise and errors from the channel.
Output (Destination): the transducer changes electrical signals into physical signals (sound, picture).

COMMUNICATION

Analogue & Digital communication

📻 Analogue communication

Transmits information using continuous signals. A message is converted into electrical signals that vary smoothly in amplitude, frequency or phase, then sent through wires, air or optical fibres.
Input transducer➜Modulator➜Channel
  • Continuous signals
  • Amplitude, frequency, phase change smoothly
  • More affected by noise and distortion
  • Traditional radio, telephone, TV

📱 Digital communication

Messages are converted into binary (0s and 1s) before transmission, sent through cables, air or fibres, then decoded back to the original form. (Have you spoken on a mobile phone?)
Source➜Encoder➜Modulator➜Channel
  • Discrete (binary) signals
  • Data sent as series of 0s and 1s
  • Less affected by noise than analogue
  • Easy compression, error detection, correction
FOCUS 1

Baseband transmission

Sending a digital signal over a channel without converting it to another frequency. The entire bandwidth of the channel is used to send one signal at a time. Signals travel through a single path from source to destination, and sending and receiving happen through the same channel.
INPUT
SIGNAL
OUTPUT
SIGNAL
ENTIRE bandwidth ➜ ONE signal at a time

Frames travel one after another on a single path, one channel at a time.

🛣️ Analogy: baseband is a single-lane road. Vehicles (frames) pass one after another, great for a short trip. The digital signal stays digital: no modulation, no frequency shifting, so no modem is needed. Sending and receiving use the same channel, which is why it is simple, cheap and fast over short distances but cannot carry many signals or travel far.

Key points

  • Uses digital signals
  • No modulation to higher frequency
  • Only one channel at a time
  • Works well for short distances only

Examples

  • Ethernet cables (LAN)
  • Communication between processor and memory
  • Telephone line
FOCUS 2

Broadband transmission

Multiple signals are transmitted simultaneously over one channel by dividing it into different frequency bands. It uses modulation to change the frequency of the signal before transmission, carries both digital and analogue signals, and is suitable for long distance. Modems do the modulation and demodulation.

Band 1 · 🎙️ VoiceBand 2 · 🗂️ DataBand 3 · 🎬 Video

⟵ frequency ⟶  Three signals share one channel at the same time, each in its own band (FDM).

🛣️ Analogy: broadband is a multi-lane highway. Step ① the sending modem modulates every signal to its own frequency band · Step ② all bands travel together on one coaxial or fibre cable · Step ③ filters at the receiver separate the bands · Step ④ the receiving modem demodulates each signal back. Because bandwidth is shared smartly, voice, data and video flow together over long distances, though signals weaken and need amplifiers or repeaters.

Working of broadband

A channel (coaxial cable or fibre optic) supports wide bandwidth. The bandwidth is split into sub-channels using Frequency-Division Multiplexing (FDM). Each signal uses a different frequency channel and is recovered with filters at the receiver.

Examples

  • Cable TV networks (many channels together)
  • Internet: DSL, cable broadband, fibre internet
  • Satellite communication
  • Mobile networks (4G/5G)
  • Wi-Fi signals

BROADBAND

Advantages & disadvantages

✅ Advantages

  • Carries multiple signals simultaneously
  • High data rate and fast speed
  • Suitable for long-distance communication
  • Supports voice, data and video at the same time

⚠️ Disadvantages

  • Complex system; needs modems and frequency filters
  • Higher cost of infrastructure
  • Signal may weaken over distance (needs amplifiers/repeaters)

Chain: digital/textual/analogue info → sampler → quantizer → coder → waveform encoder → transmitter → channel → receiver → waveform decoder → decoder → low-pass filter → output.

Digital media

Any content created, stored and shared using digital technology, viewed, listened to or interacted with on computers, smartphones and tablets. Examples: Facebook, YouTube, websites, blogs, e-books, podcasts.

DIGITAL PAYMENTS

Payments, business & entertainment

💳

Digital payments

Transactions made electronically without physical cash or cheques, through digital platforms or electronic means: faster, more convenient and often more secure. Scan QR codes or follow links.
Examples: mobile wallets (eSewa, Khalti, FonePay), online banking and mobile banking apps, debit/credit cards, POS machines, UPI-based payments, online shopping, payment gateways.

✅ Advantages

  • Fast and convenient
  • Safe, less risk of theft than carrying cash
  • Digital record of transactions
  • Useful for online shopping and services
  • Promotes a cashless economy

⚠️ Disadvantages

  • Requires internet or network access
  • Cyber-security risks if misused
  • Not suitable for people without digital literacy
🛒

Online business

Commercial activity through the internet: buying, selling, marketing, delivering services or digital information. E-commerce (Daraz, Muncha, Thulo market, Sastodeal), online services (tuition, freelancing, web designing), digital products (e-books, software, courses), affiliate marketing, advertising & content creation (YouTube, blogs).

Forms of entertainment created, delivered or enjoyed using digital devices and the internet: smartphones, tablets, smart TVs, consoles. Online games, social media (TikTok, Facebook, Instagram, reels), streaming (YouTube, Netflix, Spotify), digital music & movies, e-books, magazines, VR.
13.3

Netizenship & online reputation

🌍

Digital citizenship

Responsible and ethical use of technology, the internet and digital devices: behaving properly online, respecting others, using resources safely, following rules and laws. A person who actively uses the internet is a netizen, who sees the world as a global village.

Characteristics of a good netizen

  • Follows online etiquette; polite language
  • Respects others’ views; avoids cyberbullying and hate
  • Thinks before sharing; no false news or risky personal details
  • Follows laws, respects privacy and copyright
  • Strong passwords; avoids suspicious websites
  • Helps others and uses technology for learning
⭐

Online reputation

The image or impression a person, business or organization has on the internet, built from social media posts, comments, reviews and photos. Good: behaving responsibly, sharing meaningful content, interacting positively. Bad: harmful comments, false information, rudeness. A positive reputation affects how others perceive you, influences opportunities such as jobs or education, and builds trust.

GLOSSARY

Key terms to remember

Bit – one binary digit, 0 or 1.
Nibble / Byte – 4 bits / 8 bits.
Bandwidth – the range of frequencies a channel can carry.
Modulation – changing a signal so it suits the channel; demodulation reverses it.
Modem – modulator + demodulator; used in broadband.
FDM – splits a channel into frequency bands for many signals.
Transducer – converts one form of energy/signal to another (sound ⇄ electrical).
Sampler · Quantizer · Coder – steps that turn analogue information into binary digits.
Amplifier / Repeater – boosts a weakened signal over distance.
Netizen – a citizen who actively uses the internet.
E-commerce – buying and selling through the internet.
Cyberbullying – harassing others online; a good netizen avoids it.
Chroma key – green-screen effect in video editing.
